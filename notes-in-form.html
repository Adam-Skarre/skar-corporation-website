Mathematics as a medium

Structure made visible.

Every image on this page begins as a relationship: an angle changing over time, a radius responding to another variable, a point inheriting the state of the point before it. Code turns those rules into motion. Thousands of marks accumulate into forms that feel organic, although every position is computed.

The commercial value appears when the same visual grammar is directed toward a question. Parametric systems can expose patterns in dense data, test geometries before anything is built, translate simulation into understandable form, and give digital products an identity generated from their own logic. At that intersection, generative computation becomes a practice in data visualization, computational design, architectural form-finding, and interactive communication.

Modeling study 000

Convergence

Structure from randomness.

A Monte Carlo method estimates an answer by sampling: repeating random draws until the distribution reveals a stable pattern beneath the noise. It is how we model risk, examine ranges of possible outcomes, and stress-test decisions against uncertain futures.

Pointed at geometry instead of forecasting, the same logic produces this. Independent particles begin without order, then pass through a common rule until a probabilistic form resolves. Each cycle begins from a different sample; the governing structure remains.

This is the mathematics behind our modeling work—made visible instead of tabular.
