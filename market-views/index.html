01 · Private credit

The risk is in the loan, the funding structure, and the borrower.

A category-level label cannot substitute for analysis of debt-service capacity, collateral, seniority, documentation, sector exposure, and investor liquidity.

What the evidence establishes

Private credit is now an important source of financing for lower-rated companies. The Federal Reserve’s May 2026 Financial Stability Report estimates the market at about $1.4 trillion. It also finds that risk is uneven: debt-service capacity is weaker among some non-investment-grade public companies and riskier private firms, particularly those using floating-rate debt.

The funding model matters as much as the loan book. Traditional private-debt funds generally lock investor capital for seven to ten years. Newer semi-liquid vehicles permit periodic redemptions, subject to limits. The Federal Reserve reports $425 billion in gross assets and $241 billion in net assets across perpetual business-development companies and credit-focused interval funds—about 20% of private-credit net assets under management.

Redemption requests rose in late 2025 and accelerated in early 2026. Most affected managers limited quarterly redemptions to 5% of net asset value. The Federal Reserve described requests as manageable and reported that private-credit markets continued to function normally. Both facts matter: liquidity pressure increased, but the evidence did not support a claim of system-wide failure.

Research conclusion

Assess private credit by borrower quality, cash interest coverage, loan seniority, documentation, sector concentration, vintage, and the liquidity promised to investors. Broad labels conceal the variables most likely to determine loss.

02 · AI infrastructure

Computing growth is colliding with the slow timetable of electricity infrastructure.

Demand for computing can rise quickly. Generation, transmission, interconnection, substations, cooling systems, and construction capacity do not.

What the evidence establishes

The U.S. Energy Information Administration reports that national electricity demand grew about 1.7% annually between 2020 and 2025, after growing only 0.1% annually between 2005 and 2019. It identifies data-center electricity use as a major source of the change.

The range of plausible outcomes remains wide. In the EIA’s 2026 long-term cases, electricity used by data-center servers reaches between 446 and 818 billion kilowatthours in 2050. That spread is important: long-range demand is a scenario, not an observed fact. Investment decisions should therefore distinguish requested capacity, contracted capacity, powered capacity, and economically utilized capacity.

The constraint varies by location. A project may have suitable land and computing equipment but lack a timely grid connection; another may have power but face equipment or construction delays. Aggregate demand forecasts cannot reveal which constraint controls a specific project.

Research conclusion

Evaluate AI infrastructure at the project level. Use signed customer commitments, connection dates, deliverable power, construction schedules, utilization, revenue per unit of capacity, and financing terms—not announced megawatts alone.

03 · Public markets

Valuation and earnings answer different questions.

A high price relative to expected earnings increases sensitivity to disappointment. It does not, by itself, establish that earnings are fictitious or that a reversal is imminent.

What the evidence establishes

The Federal Reserve’s May 2026 review found that the forward price-to-earnings ratio of S&P 500 companies remained near the upper end of its historical distribution. It also found that the estimated equity premium remained well below its historical average. Those observations indicate limited compensation for bearing risk and a demanding level of expected execution.

At the same time, the Bureau of Economic Analysis estimated first-quarter 2026 corporate profits from current production at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of $4,426.5 billion, up from $4,352.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. Aggregate profits therefore do not support the claim that the market rests on no current income. They also cannot establish whether a particular security is fairly priced.

A defensible view requires company-level analysis of revenue concentration, operating margins, reinvestment needs, cash conversion, balance-sheet obligations, and the growth already embedded in the price.

Research conclusion

Separate the existence of profits from the price paid for them. The relevant test is whether future cash flows can meet the growth, margin, and duration assumptions implied by the current valuation.
