VIEW 01BUSINESS TECHNOLOGY
28 JUL 2026

AI advantage will be won in workflow depth, not tool count.

Current U.S. business data shows adoption remains uneven by firm size and sector. Among users, deployment is still concentrated in a limited number of business functions.

Evidence

Census BTOS data placed current business AI use near 17–20% during the latest collection windows. A related Census working paper found 57% of adopting firms used AI in three or fewer business functions.

Decision implication

Start with one consequential workflow. Establish its baseline, decision owner, controls, and measure of value before expanding access across the organization.

What would change our view

Broad, multi-function adoption accompanied by durable operating gains across smaller firms—not higher stated intent or tool access alone.

VIEW 02MANUFACTURING SYSTEMS
28 JUL 2026

A dashboard is an output of readiness—not a substitute for it.

NIST’s manufacturing-readiness work evaluates organizational maturity, information technology, performance management, and connectivity. A visual layer alone cannot resolve weaknesses in those underlying systems.

Evidence

The NIST Smart Manufacturing Systems Readiness Level tool is use-case driven and assesses multiple operational dimensions before technology selection and implementation.

Decision implication

Define ownership, data meaning, system boundaries, and the decision each measure supports before purchasing integration or dashboard software.

What would change our view

A pilot that produces traceable, repeatable decisions from existing data without separate work on definitions, ownership, or connectivity.

VIEW 03ENERGY & INFRASTRUCTURE
28 JUL 2026

Load growth should be planned as a range with gates, not a single number.

DOE estimates U.S. data-center electricity use could reach 325–580 TWh by 2028. The width of that range is itself decision-relevant: it argues for staged commitments and explicit triggers.

Evidence

DOE reported 176 TWh of U.S. data-center electricity use in 2023 and a 2028 range equal to roughly 6.7–12% of national electricity consumption.

Decision implication

Separate no-regret enabling work from irreversible capacity commitments. Tie each stage to contracted load, interconnection progress, and verified regional supply.

What would change our view

Project-specific demand commitments and deliverable capacity narrow the range enough that delaying a major commitment costs more than preserving flexibility.
